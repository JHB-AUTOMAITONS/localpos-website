What a work order is

A work order is a written instruction to carry out a job. It names the party doing the work, describes the work, fixes the price and the dates, and is signed by both sides. Once accepted, it is the paper both parties go back to when a question comes up.

It usually moves in one of two directions. A customer issues it to a contractor after accepting a quotation, as in the first sample. Or a service business writes it up for its own team when a customer hands over a job, which is what a repair shop’s job card does.

Without one, a job runs on phone calls and memory. The scope grows, the completion date slides and the final bill becomes an argument. One signed page prevents most of that.

What goes into a work order format

Six groups of information cover almost every job, large or small.

Customer information
Who is ordering the work, the site address, and the person at the site who can take decisions.
Contractor or assignee
The firm or person doing the work, with GSTIN if registered and a contact number.
Work description
One line that names the job, plus a reference to the quotation, drawing or estimate it is based on.
Scope of work
A table of tasks with unit, quantity and rate. What is not written here is not part of the job.
Price
The value of the work, tax shown separately, and the total. State whether materials are included or supplied by the customer.
Timeline
Start date and completion date. For a longer job, add dates for the main stages.
Payment milestones
The advance and the stages at which further payments fall due, as a percentage and as an amount.
Terms and signatures
Extra work, working hours, safety, warranty and delay, followed by the signatures of both parties.

Work order and purchase order: which one to use

Both are orders placed with another business. The difference lies in what is being ordered.

PointWork orderPurchase order
Used forServices and jobsGoods
Heart of the documentScope of work and timelineItems, quantities and rates

Ordering goods only? The purchase order generator prepares that document. When a job includes both supply and labour, one work order that lists both is normally enough.

Businesses that run on work orders

  • Contractors

    Civil, electrical, plumbing and interior jobs where the scope and the payment stages must be agreed before work starts.

  • Fabricators

    Gates, grills, sheds and machine parts made to a drawing, with material weight and rate written down.

  • AMC providers

    Annual maintenance contracts that list the equipment covered, the number of visits and the response time.

  • Repair shops

    Mobile, appliance and vehicle repairs, where a job order format records the complaint, the estimate and the promised date.

  • Printers

    Print jobs with size, paper, quantity, colours and delivery date fixed before the plates are made.

  • Tailors and boutiques

    Made-to-order work where measurements, fabric and trial dates need to be on paper.

How a job moves from enquiry to payment

  1. 01

    Estimate or quote

    The contractor studies the job and sends a figure. For an approximate cost use the estimate format. For a fixed offer use the quotation maker.

  2. 02

    Issue the work order

    Copy the agreed scope, price and dates into the work order, add the payment milestones and terms, and have both sides sign.

  3. 03

    Track the work

    Record changes as written additions, with a date and a price, instead of spoken instructions at the site.

  4. 04

    Bill and close

    Raise an invoice at each milestone and mark the order closed at handover. For daily invoicing, LocalPOS GST billing software numbers each bill and keeps the customer’s payment history.

Who issues a work order?

Usually the customer issues it to the contractor after accepting a quotation. Service and repair businesses also prepare one internally, often called a job order or job card, when they accept a job from a customer.

Is a work order a contract?

Once it is issued and accepted, it records what both sides agreed and is commonly relied on as the agreement for the job. For large or risky jobs, a detailed contract drafted with legal advice is the safer course.

How do I download the work order template?

Choose a layout above, then pick Excel (.xlsx) or Word (.docx). You can take it with the sample values or blank. To get a PDF, print it and choose “Save as PDF” in the print window.

What payment milestones are common?

There is no fixed rule. An advance, one or two stage payments and a final payment on completion is a common pattern. In the sample it is 30%, 40% and 30%. Set the stages to match when the contractor spends on materials.

Should GST be shown on a work order?

If the contractor is registered and will charge GST, show the value before tax, the tax and the total, so the order value matches the invoices that follow. The rate depends on the nature of the work. Confirm it with a chartered accountant.

What if the scope changes after the order is signed?

Write the change down as an amendment or an additional order with its own price and effect on the completion date, and have both parties sign it. Do not rely on a spoken instruction.
