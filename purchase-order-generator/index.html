What a purchase order does

A purchase order, or PO, is the buyer’s written instruction to a supplier. It records three things: what was ordered, at what price, and by when it should arrive.

Many small shops order by phone or WhatsApp, and that works until a carton turns up short or the bill shows a higher rate than the one agreed. A PO gives both sides one page to point to. When the supplier’s invoice arrives, you compare it with the order line by line.

Unlike a sales bill, a PO is not a tax document. The supplier’s tax invoice is what supports your input tax credit, so the PO does not replace it.

From purchase order to payment

The usual sequence in a trading or retail business.

  1. 01

    You issue the PO

    Send the order to the supplier and ask them to confirm it. Keep a copy with its PO number.

  2. 02

    The supplier dispatches

    Goods arrive with the supplier’s invoice, and sometimes with a delivery challan when the invoice follows later. Both should quote your PO number.

  3. 03

    You receive and check

    Count the goods against the PO and inspect the quality. Note shortages, damage and wrong items on the supplier’s copy before signing.

  4. 04

    You pay

    Pay as per the agreed terms once order, goods and invoice match. For rejected or short goods, a debit note records the amount you are deducting.

Description, HSN or SAC code, quantity, unit rate, discount % and GST % for each line, up to 50 lines.
GST type
CGST + SGST when the supplier is in your state, IGST when they are in another state, or No GST when the supplier is unregistered or under composition.
Payment terms
How and when you will pay, for example “30 days from receipt of goods”.

Where a written order earns its place

  • Stocking up for a season

    A garment shop in Surat ordering festival stock from four suppliers can see every commitment and its delivery date.

  • Settling a rate dispute

    When the bill shows ₹52 a piece and you agreed ₹48, the PO settles it without a long call.

  • When staff place orders

    The owner approves one page, and the person at the receiving counter knows what should arrive.

Purchase order questions

Is the purchase order generator free?

Yes. There is no charge and no sign-up. It runs in your browser and the details you type are not uploaded.

Is a purchase order legally binding?

A PO is an offer to buy. Once the supplier accepts it, by confirming or by supplying against it, it generally works as an agreement on the terms written in it. For large orders, have the terms reviewed by a lawyer.

Do I need to show GST on a PO?

It is not a tax document, but showing the expected GST helps you see the full cost and helps the supplier bill you under the right tax head. The tax that counts is the one on the supplier’s invoice.

What is the difference between a PO and a work order?

A purchase order is usually for goods at a quantity and rate. A work order instructs someone to carry out a job, such as fabrication, repair or installation, and describes the work and timeline. See the work order format for that.

Can I change a PO after sending it?

Yes, if the supplier agrees. Issue a revised order with the same PO number and a revision mark such as “Rev 1”, and ask the supplier to confirm it, so that only one version is in force.

How do I keep track of many open orders?

This PO generator makes one document at a time and remembers none of them. When you have several suppliers and regular orders, purchase management in LocalPOS records purchases against suppliers and adds the received stock to inventory.
