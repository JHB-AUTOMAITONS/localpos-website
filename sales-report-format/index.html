How the daily and monthly sheets are built

Both sheets answer the same question, how much did we sell, but at a different level of detail.

Daily sheet: one row per bill

  • The date, the first and last bill number, and the number of bills raised.
  • One row for each bill, or one row for each item if you prefer an item-wise day book.
  • The amount of each bill placed under cash, UPI, card or credit.
  • Total sales, returns for the day and net sales.
  • A cash tally that ends with the closing cash in the drawer.

Monthly sheet: one row per day or week

  • The month, the financial year and the number of trading days.
  • One row for each day, or for each week when you want a single page.
  • Bills, gross sales, returns and discounts, and net sales in every row.
  • The GST collected, kept apart from your own sales figure.
  • A total row, which becomes the starting point for your books.

Summary fields in a sales report format

These are the figures at the foot of the sheets above. Use the same meaning every day, or the totals stop being comparable.

Total sales (gross sales)
The value of all bills raised in the period, before returns are taken off. Decide once whether you record it with or without GST and stay with that choice.
Returns and discounts
Goods taken back and price reductions given after billing. They are shown separately so that a high figure is noticed.
Net sales
Total sales less returns and discounts. This is the figure that goes to the top of your income statement.
Payment split
Net or total sales divided by how the customer paid: cash, UPI, card or credit. Only the cash column should match the drawer.
Credit sales
Bills that are not yet paid. They are sales today, but the money arrives later, so they need follow-up.
GST
The tax collected on the sales of the period. It is not your income. It is payable to the government after input tax credit.
Closing cash
Opening cash plus cash received, less refunds, cash expenses and bank deposits. It should equal the cash counted at closing time.

Who uses a sales report

  • Shop owners

    To see the day’s sales at closing time and to check that the cash in the drawer matches the report.

  • Sales staff and managers

    To compare days, weeks and counters, and to notice a slow week before the month is over.

  • Accountants

    To post monthly sales and GST into the books without opening every bill again.

  • Lenders and partners

    A bank or a business partner may ask for month-wise sales as a quick picture of how the business is doing.

A simple routine for keeping the sheets

  1. 01

    Fill the daily sheet at closing

    Enter each bill from the bill book, or copy the totals from your billing machine, and put every amount under the right payment column.

  2. 02

    Count the cash

    Work out the closing cash from the tally and compare it with the cash in the drawer. Write down any difference the same day.

  3. 03

    Carry totals to the month

    Copy the day’s bills, gross sales, returns and net sales to the monthly sheet. In Excel you can add a formula for the total row.

  4. 04

    Review at month end

    Compare the month with the previous one, look at returns and credit, and hand the totals to your accountant.

When the sheet takes too long to fill

A daily sales report format in Excel works well while you raise a few dozen bills a day. Past that, copying bills into a sheet every evening takes time, and one missed bill throws off the cash tally.

Questions about sales reports

What should a daily sales report include?

The date, the bills raised during the day, the amount of each bill, how it was paid (cash, UPI, card or credit), total sales, returns and net sales. A cash tally with the closing cash is useful for any shop that handles cash.

Should the report be bill-wise or item-wise?

Bill-wise is quicker and is enough to check cash and payment modes. Item-wise shows what sold, which helps with reordering. Many shops keep the day bill-wise and add a category or item summary once a month, like the third sheet on this page.

Is there a monthly sales report format in Excel here?

Yes. Choose the monthly sheet above and download it as an Excel workbook (.xlsx) or a Word document (.docx), with the sample figures or blank. It is free and needs no sign-up. To get a PDF, print it and choose “Save as PDF”.

Should sales be shown with GST or without GST?

Either can work, as long as you are consistent and the heading says which one it is. The monthly sample shows sales before GST and the tax in its own column, which makes it easier to hand the figures to an accountant.

How is average bill value calculated?

Divide total sales by the number of bills for the same period. In the daily sample, 11,000 ÷ 8 bills gives an average bill value of ₹1,375.

Why does my closing cash not match the report?

The usual causes are a UPI or card bill entered as cash, a cash expense or refund that was not written down, or wrong change given. Check the payment column of each bill first, then the expenses and deposits.
